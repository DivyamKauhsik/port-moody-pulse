Port Moody in brief

A small city with a big sense of itself.

Port Moody sits at the eastern end of Burrard Inlet, where the water meets the Coast Mountains. It is the smallest of the Tri-Cities — Port Moody, Coquitlam, and Port Coquitlam — and the one most people describe first as a feeling: a waterfront, a main street, and neighbours who know each other.

The city calls itself the City of the Arts, and it shows: galleries, studios, and public art sit alongside the old rail town on Clarke Street, the breweries on Murray Street, and the trails that run from the inlet up into the mountains.

Neighbourhoods run from Newport Village and Suter Brook on the waterfront, through Moody Centre and Glenayre, up to Heritage Mountain, College Park, and Pleasantside, and east to Ioco.

City Hall, translated

The new council has taken its seats.

Port Moody has a new mayor, Haven Lurbiecki, elected October 17 with 5,400 votes. The new council — Paul Lambert, Callan Morrison, Amy Lubik, Kyla Knowles, Peter Thomasen, and Alexandra Chan — was sworn in on November 3, 2026.

On its first night, council split 4–3 over regional appointments, amending four-year terms down to 2026–2027, and named Thomasen as the Police Board representative. Most advisory committees are paused pending review; the Finance Committee carries on, with Morrison as chair and Lambert as vice-chair.

Provincially, John McEwen won Port Moody–Burquitlam on the final count, 11,929 votes to Rick Glumac’s 10,760; the official declaration is expected by November 10.

Things to do

A first-timer’s Port Moody.

If someone visits for a day, this is where you’d send them.

Rocky Point Park

The waterfront front yard: trails, the pier, the pool and spray park in summer, and the best sunset spot on the inlet.

Shoreline Trail

The flat, stroller-friendly walk from Rocky Point around the inlet — herons, seals, and the old mill site along the way.

Brewer’s Row

The craft breweries along Murray Street, a short walk from Moody Centre Station. An afternoon, not a crawl.

PoMoArts

The Port Moody Arts Centre on Kyle Street: exhibitions, classes, and the gallery at the heart of the City of the Arts.

Clarke Street & the Station Museum

The old rail town: the 1908 station museum, the shops and cafés along Clarke, and the Saturday market in season.

Getting here

SkyTrain Millennium Line to Moody Centre or Inlet Centre Station, or the West Coast Express to Moody Centre. No car needed.

Good to know

Questions people ask about Port Moody.

What is Port Moody known for?

Port Moody is known as the City of the Arts, for its Burrard Inlet waterfront and Rocky Point Park, and for Brewer’s Row, the cluster of craft breweries on Murray Street. It is the smallest of the Tri-Cities, at the eastern end of Burrard Inlet.

What is there to do in Port Moody?

Walk the Shoreline Trail, spend an afternoon at Rocky Point Park, visit the breweries on Murray Street, see a show at PoMoArts or the Inlet Theatre, and check the Port Moody Pulse events page for markets, concerts, and seasonal events like the Christmas lights map.

How do I get to Port Moody by transit?

Take the SkyTrain Millennium Line to Moody Centre Station or Inlet Centre Station. The West Coast Express also stops at Moody Centre Station.

When is the Port Moody Christmas lights map?

The Christmas lights map runs through the season, with Lights at Lafarge opening November 27 and the CPKC Holiday Train stopping in Port Moody on December 20. The full map is on the Christmas lights map page.
